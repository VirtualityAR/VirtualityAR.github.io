In [[Sanskrit]], compound words (**samāsa**) are heavily used in sutras. How you break the sandhi determines the type of compound, which directly dictates the gender of the word.

**Tatpuruṣa vs. Bahuvrīhi**:

- If you split a compound as a **Tatpuruṣa** (noun + noun), the final compound takes the gender of the _last word_.
- If you split it as a **Bahuvrīhi** (an adjective-like compound describing someone else), the entire compound shifts its gender to match the _person or object it is describing_.


**Why Commentaries Matter**

Because one string of sandhi letters can be split in multiple grammatically valid ways, ancient scholars wrote massive commentaries (_[[Bhāṣya]]s_) just to debate where the word breaks should be. A single split can alter the entire philosophical direction of a school of thought.
